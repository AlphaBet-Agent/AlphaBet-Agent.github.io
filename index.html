02 The idea · longitudinal forecasting training

Forecast one question on multiple dates.
One outcome scores them all.

AlphaBet revisits one question on several dates, each time searching only the news published by then. Each dated score is a training reward for the shared policy.

1,028→3,965
events → question-dates

Every question-date is its own training sample: one resolved event, 3.9 samples on average, no extra labels.

memory-free

Every scheduled date starts fresh: the agent searches the dated corpus again and reports again. Nothing is carried from one date to the next.

memory-on

With each forecast the agent also writes a belief notebook: its probabilities, supporting evidence, open questions. Only the notebook is carried to the next date; everything else starts fresh.

03 Two propositions

Why one outcome is enough, and why the mean is enough.

Proposition 1 · agentic forecast properness

With a strictly proper score and no outcome leakage, expected regret is exactly an information gap plus a reporting gap.

regret = E[ Σ_t ( D_S(q_t, q_t^H) + D_S(q_t^H, p_t) ) ]
q
posterior given the full dated corpus
qH
posterior given what the agent retrieved
p
what the agent reported

Zero regret exactly when p = qH = q at every date. One outcome scores the sum of both gaps.

Proposition 2 · GRPO without std

Mean-centering only rescales the update of the proper score. Dividing by the group’s spread tilts its target.

q̃ᴴ(k) = [qᴴ(k)/(σ_k+ε)] / Σ_j [qᴴ(j)/(σ_j+ε)]

mean-centered · AlphaBet

A = R − mean(R)

Same direction, scaled by 1 − 1/G.

standardized · vanilla GRPO

A = (R − mean(R)) / (σ + ε)

As G grows, the target tilts to q̃H unless every σk is equal.

04 Analysis

Where the gains come from, and what breaks calibration.

Every date beats one date, most of all far from settlement.

Same 1,028 events; one forecast date per event, or all of them. The gap widens farther from settlement.

0.668→0.626
Brier · single-date → memory-free

Better evidence seeker,
better reporter.

Freeze the articles each agent retrieved and hand them to tool-free readers. Swapping the reader moves Brier about four times as much as swapping the evidence, and AlphaBet’s evidence helps every reader.

Standardization keeps the accuracy and breaks properness.

The same training with and without GRPO’s standard-deviation step. With it, both proper scores come out worse and the policy grows over-confident; accuracy remains comparable.

05 In short

Learn from every day
an event stayed open.

AlphaBet is an outcome-based RL recipe for agentic forecasting: one policy, many dated information states, one proper-score reward per date, and GRPO without standard-deviation normalization, so the reward keeps its scale.

01

Supervise every date.

A resolved event properly scores each pre-resolution forecast, turning 1,028 events into 3,965 training states.

02

Train both halves.

The gains come from better evidence and better probabilities: AlphaBet’s retrieved articles help every reader, and AlphaBet reads the same articles better.

03

Mind the normalization.

Standard-deviation normalization tilts the proper-score target and over-sharpens forecasts; mean-centering alone keeps the incentive honest.
